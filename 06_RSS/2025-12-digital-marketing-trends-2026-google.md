# Top Digital Marketing Trends and Predictions for 2026

**Author:** Phil Wilson, Vice President of Ads Marketing, EMEA, Google

**Publication Date:** December 2025

**Reading Time:** 5 min

Phil Wilson, Vice President of Ads Marketing for Europe, the Middle East, and Africa at Google, examines emerging marketing trends anticipated for 2026.

## Introduction

As the year concludes, Wilson reflects on what lies ahead, noting that 2026 will bring continued AI progress and technological innovation. Despite these changes, consumers remain focused on finding answers, products, and connections through searching, streaming, scrolling, and shopping. According to a Google/Ipsos survey, "83% of global consumers report using Google and/or YouTube on a daily basis," underscoring the scale at which these platforms understand customer intent.

## Five Key Trends

### 1. People Prioritize Present Wellbeing

Consumers increasingly experience anxiety and fatigue, shifting focus toward immediate gratification rather than distant goals. This trend reflects a rational response to economic uncertainty, particularly among younger generations. Brands should "deconstruct your value proposition" and emphasize smaller, instantly rewarding milestones rather than ultimate outcomes.

### 2. AI Transforms Consumer Behavior

AI is reshaping how people search, moving beyond fact-finding toward "dynamic exploration." Consumers now expect brands to provide "tangible, visual answers" to their queries. Marketers should adopt "Generative Engine Optimisation" by creating authoritative, people-first content that works effectively within AI-powered conversational search.

### 3. Young Audiences Seek Creative Participation

Today's digitally-native audiences desire to participate in and remix brand stories rather than passively consume them. The movement toward "creative maximalism" means brands should partner with creators and provide raw materials for audience co-creation, transforming the relationship from storytelling to audiences into world-building with them.

### 4. The Rise of the Nostalgic Remix

Nostalgia has become an economic driver, with nostalgic campaigns proven to increase brand likability. Rather than simply re-releasing old content, successful brands strategically "remix" intellectual property by collaborating with partners to create fresh experiences that feel both contemporary and comfortingly familiar.

### 5. The Future of Sustainability Is Tangible Value

The era of vague sustainability pledges has ended. Brands must shift from broad environmental claims to highlighting specific, measurable product benefits like durability or energy efficiency, demonstrating genuine consumer value rather than corporate virtue.

## Conclusion

Wilson emphasizes that understanding consumer intent at scale—through searching, streaming, scrolling, and shopping—remains central to effective 2026 marketing strategies.
