## 全文

# The PMS Era is Ending

**Author:** Markus Busch, Editor/Publisher at hospitality.today

**Publication Date:** May 13, 2026

The property management system era is concluding because the industry's focus has fundamentally shifted away from internal operations toward external commerce coordination. According to the author, "Operations is no longer where the property-level system matters most. Commerce is."

## The Consolidation Strategy's Flaw

Major PMS vendors are pursuing aggressive acquisition strategies, combining revenue management systems, channel managers, payment processors, and booking engines into unified platforms. However, this approach faces a critical challenge: speed. The author explains that "The new protocols ship monthly. The new architectures appear in production before they appear in conference keynotes."

Unified suites operate on annual release cycles, while specialized vendors can deploy updates as quickly as new protocols become available. This temporal mismatch disadvantages consolidated platforms in rapidly evolving markets.

## Four Transforming Commercial Functions

**Distribution:** AI agents are reshaping the booking funnel. Companies like Sabre and The Hotels Network are leading innovation by focusing exclusively on distribution, enabling faster adaptation than PMS bundled modules.

**Customer Relationship Management:** Modern CRM systems are transitioning from rule-based triggers to vector-based architectures that identify patterns across millions of signals. Specialists like Bookboost and Revinate are rebuilding around AI-driven personalization.

**Revenue Management:** Dynamic pricing now must account for AI agents evaluating hundreds of properties simultaneously. Specialists including Duetto and IDeaS are implementing machine-learning approaches rather than traditional rule-based systems.

**Payments:** New payment infrastructure specifically designed for AI-to-AI transactions is emerging from technology giants like Stripe, AWS, and Google—capabilities PMS vendors cannot independently replicate.

## Marketplace Integration Limitations

While unified suites offer marketplace integrations with specialist vendors, the author contends these connections lack meaningful depth. "Most marketplace integrations are interval-based data syncs that conform to the PMS's data model and ship on the PMS's release cadence."

This architectural constraint undermines specialist capabilities. A vector-based CRM connected through nightly synchronization essentially functions as a slower rule-based system. Real-time pricing updates become hourly updates when routed through PMS infrastructure.

## The Reimagined PMS

The future PMS functions as a commerce hub rather than an all-encompassing platform. The author describes this transformed role: "The PMS becomes the property's commerce hub—the layer where every specialist's output converges."

The PMS responsibilities narrow to three core functions: maintaining authoritative data, executing transactions, and integrating third-party outputs into unified reporting dashboards. Each specialist layer operates at its own pace while feeding into centralized decision-making infrastructure.

## Strategic Implications

Hoteliers selecting technology stacks need systems designed for continuous change rather than stability. The author concludes that "The PMS era ended when the focus moved away from operations. The next era belongs to operators who notice—and to the systems that can keep moving while the world around them keeps changing."
